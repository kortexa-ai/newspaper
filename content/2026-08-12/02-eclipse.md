title: The Moon crosses Spain today, for the first time in 121 years
kicker: Space
author: The Editors
summary: A total solar eclipse sweeps Greenland, Iceland, and mainland Spain today — the first to touch Spanish soil since 1905 — with observatories and space agencies streaming it live for anyone outside the path.

---

Later today, the Moon's shadow will race across Greenland, Iceland, and
then down into Spain and northeastern Portugal, giving mainland Spain its
first total solar eclipse since 1905. Totality lasts only a few minutes
along the path, but the run-up to it has turned into one of the more
democratized astronomy events of the year.

The European Space Agency is broadcasting live from the Observatorio
Astrofísico de Javalambre in Teruel, Spain, with the feed running
19:30–20:45 CEST on ESA Web TV and YouTube, hosted by space scientist
Maggie Aderin alongside ESA's Director of Science, Carole Mundell
([ESA](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/Join_ESA_for_a_total_solar_eclipse_on_12_August_2026)).
On the ground, the city of León is hosting a free public observation
program with expert talks and workshops for anyone who wants to watch in
person rather than online. ESA also built a bilingual educational kit on
safe eclipse viewing for teachers and students across Europe.

None of this is new science — eclipses are among astronomy's most
predictable events — but the coordination behind it is a small triumph of
its own: an agency, a regional observatory, and a city government lining
up to put a rare celestial event in front of as many people as possible,
safely, for free. For everyone outside the path of totality, NASA is also
compiling contributions from citizen scientists and public livestreams
into its own coverage ([NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/eclipses/future-eclipses/total-solar-eclipse-on-august-12-2026/)).
Anyone watching in person is reminded: certified eclipse glasses only —
direct viewing, even of a mostly covered Sun, can damage eyesight.
