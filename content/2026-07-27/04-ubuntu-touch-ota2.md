title: Ubuntu Touch's phone OS gets its biggest update in years
kicker: Open Source
author: The Editors
summary: OTA 2.0 rebases the volunteer-run mobile Linux project on Ubuntu 24.04, adds new phone support, and quietly keeps an alternative to iOS and Android alive.

---

The UBports Foundation shipped Ubuntu Touch OTA 2.0 this week, the second
major release built on the Ubuntu 24.04 LTS base and the most substantial
update to the open-source mobile OS in some time
([9to5Linux](https://9to5linux.com/ubuntu-touch-ota-2-0-officially-released-for-supported-linux-phones-and-tablets);
corroborated by [Tux Machines](https://news.tuxmachines.org/n/2026/07/24/Ubuntu_Touch_OTA_2_0_Officially_Released_for_Supported_Linux_Ph.shtml)
and [LinuxCompatible](https://www.linuxcompatible.org/story/ubports-ships-ubuntu-touch-ota-20-with-chromium-134-and-2404-lts)).

The update brings the Morph Browser up to Chromium 134, a meaningful jump
for compatibility with a modern web that has mostly moved on from older
rendering engines, plus a Widevine installer for apps that need
DRM-protected video. New device support lands for the Nothing Phone (1)
and the Zinwa Q25, extending a hardware list that has always depended on
volunteers reverse-engineering support for phones never designed with
Ubuntu Touch in mind. Smaller but real quality-of-life work fills out the
rest: a built-in screenshot editor for cropping and color-adjusting
images, an expanded emoji keyboard, Georgian-language keyboard support,
and user data encryption promoted to stable on devices that support it.

None of this will move Ubuntu Touch's tiny market share. What it does is
keep a genuine alternative alive — a phone operating system with no
ad-tracking business model, built and maintained by people who show up
because they want a third option to exist between two giants. OTA 2.0 is
proof that showing up, release after release, is still working.
