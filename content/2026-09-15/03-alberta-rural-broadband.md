Rural broadband expansion rarely makes for dramatic news, one funding announcement and one county at a time. But it's one of the more reliable forms of progress going — connectivity, once built, tends to stay built — and this week's installment came from Alberta, where the federal and provincial governments jointly committed $24.8 million to bring high-speed internet to 1,833 rural and remote households ([The Stettler Independent](https://stettlerindependent.com/2026/09/14/joint-24-8-million-investment-to-bring-broadband-to-1833-rural-homes/)).

The money is a new slice of a much larger $780-million broadband agreement Ottawa and Alberta signed back in March 2022, aimed at closing a gap that, at the time, left roughly 201,000 households in the province without reliable high-speed access. The program's track record so far is substantial: $646 million already committed across 82 projects, with 21 finished — bringing service to 20,600 households — 46 more under construction, and 15 in planning. In total, the province says 137,100 households have gained access since the agreement began, with another 93,700 expected to connect through related federal programs.

"Every corner of Alberta should have access to dependable, affordable high-speed internet," said Buckley Belanger, federal secretary of state for rural development, pointing to communities like Swan Hills and Buck Lake among those set to benefit from this latest round. Alberta's technology minister, Nate Glubish, framed the investment in practical terms: reliable internet for rural communities means better access to telehealth, remote work and online learning, and support for local businesses trying to compete beyond their immediate area. The province estimates the broader strategy will ultimately generate around 2,000 jobs.

Ottawa's stated goal is 100 percent high-speed access for Canadian households by 2030. Alberta's numbers suggest that's less a distant target than a project already well underway — one rural exchange, one set of homes, one funding round at a time.
