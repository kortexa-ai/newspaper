title: A Wisconsin utility just put its money behind a fusion startup's reactor
kicker: Climate
author: The Editors
summary: Madison Gas and Electric took an equity stake in Realta Fusion and will help site, permit, and finance a proposed 200-megawatt magnetic-mirror fusion plant on Madison's old Oscar Mayer site.

---

Fusion startups have mostly raised money from venture funds and
governments. This week, Realta Fusion announced something different: a
strategic partnership with Madison Gas and Electric, the utility that
actually serves the city where the company is based, including a direct
equity investment from MGE alongside engineering, siting, permitting,
interconnection, and financing support
([PR
Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/realta-fusion--madison-gas-and-electric-announce-strategic-partnership-to-explore-200-mwe-fusion-energy-power-plant-in-wisconsin-302865022.html);
[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/03/utilities-are-racing-to-link-up-with-fusion-startups-with-realta-fusion-the-latest-to-benefit/)).

The partnership sets up exploration of a 200-megawatt fusion plant using
Realta's magnetic-mirror approach, and gives Realta a home for the work:
a roughly 250,000-square-foot research and development facility, dubbed
the Realta Forge, at OM Station, the former Oscar Mayer meatpacking site
in MGE's own service territory. For MGE, which has committed to net-zero
carbon electricity by 2050, the deal is a bet on a technology still years
from generating a single watt commercially. For Realta — one of eight
companies in the Department of Energy's Milestone-Based Fusion
Development Program and a 2026 World Economic Forum Technology
Pioneer — it's a utility willing to put engineering staff and site access
behind an early-stage reactor design, the kind of practical, on-the-ground
support that's harder to buy than capital alone.

It's a study and a siting effort, not a groundbreaking. But a utility
choosing to embed itself this early, in its own backyard, is its own kind
of signal about where it expects the grid to get its power from next.
