---
title: Windows 11 will now just work with a braille display, no setup needed
kicker: Accessibility
author: The Editors
summary: A new Insider build lets Narrator recognize any HID-standard braille display the moment it's plugged in — including during the very first screen of Windows setup.
order: 4
---

Microsoft's latest Windows 11 Insider build, 28020.2539, released July
20, makes a small change with an outsized effect for blind and
deaf-blind users: refreshable braille displays now connect to Narrator
instantly, with no drivers or configuration required
([Microsoft Learn, official release notes](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows-insider/release-notes/beta-26h1/preview-build-28020-2539)).
Narrator now recognizes any display built to the HID standard, an open
specification for braille hardware — plug one in over USB, or pair it
over Bluetooth the same way you would a mouse or headset, and it works.
Confirmed compatible devices include the Orbit Reader 20, Orbit Slate
340, Freedom Scientific Focus 40, and APH Mantis Q40.

The detail that matters most is where this support now reaches: HID
braille displays work during the initial Windows setup experience
itself, when a PC is first turned on and no accounts or settings exist
yet. That has historically been one of the least accessible moments in
owning a computer, requiring sighted help to get through before any
assistive technology could be configured. A deaf-blind user can now
complete that first screen independently, with their own display, before
anything else about the machine has been set up.

The build also expands Voice Access to Portuguese and Korean and adds
gamepad navigation to Bluetooth quick settings. Insider builds are
previews, not guarantees — Microsoft cautions that features can change
before reaching everyone — but plug-and-play braille support, especially
at setup, is the kind of unglamorous fix that quietly removes a barrier
for people who've had to route around it for years.
