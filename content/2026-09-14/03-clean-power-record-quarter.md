The American Clean Power Association's latest quarterly market report, out this week, has the kind of numbers that are easy to skim past and worth stopping for: 17.1 gigawatts of utility-scale solar, land-based wind and battery storage came online in the second quarter of 2026 — the strongest second quarter, and the strongest first half, the industry has recorded. That's a 45% jump over the same quarter last year, split across 7.4 gigawatts of solar, nearly 5 gigawatts of wind and 4,685 megawatts (17,177 megawatt-hours) of storage spread over 164 separate projects ([PV Tech](https://www.pv-tech.org/us-adds-17-1gw-of-clean-power-capacity-in-record-q2-acp-reports/); [Windtech International](https://www.windtech-international.com/industry-news/us-clean-power-additions-reach-17-1-gw-in-second-quarter)).

Cumulative clean power capacity in the U.S. now stands at roughly 387,600 megawatts — enough, ACP estimates, to power about 83 million homes. New Mexico led all states for the quarter, adding more than 4 gigawatts and ending Texas's two-and-a-half-year run at the top; Pattern Energy's 3.65-gigawatt SunZia Wind project, one of the largest wind farms ever built in the country, accounted for most of that. The build-out pipeline behind all this also grew, reaching a record 204.6 gigawatts of projects in development. "When clean energy is allowed to compete fairly with other technologies, we know that it can deliver reliable, affordable energy to power American homes and businesses," said JC Sandberg, the association's chief policy officer.

None of this is a single breakthrough moment — it's the unglamorous compounding of thousands of smaller projects, quarter after quarter, adding up to a grid that looks different than it did even two years ago.
