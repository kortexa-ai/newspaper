---
title: The Ocean Cleanup is building its biggest river barrier yet, for a very specific problem
kicker: Climate
author: The Editors
summary: The nonprofit and the Dominican Republic are replacing a six-year-old trash interceptor on the Ozama River with a 400-meter barrier built to handle the seasonal water hyacinth blooms that keep choking Santo Domingo's waterway.
order: 4
---

Since 2020, a single floating barrier called Interceptor 004 has kept more
than 300 tonnes of waste out of the Caribbean Sea by catching it as it
drifts down the Ozama River through Santo Domingo. The Ocean Cleanup and the
Dominican Republic government announced this week that they are replacing
it with something considerably bigger: a 400-meter barrier, the largest the
organization has ever built, split into two overlapping sections that span
the river's full width while leaving a channel open for boat traffic
([The Ocean Cleanup](https://theoceancleanup.com/press/)).

The upgrade is aimed at a problem the original interceptor was never quite
built for. Each year, the Ozama fills with dense mats of invasive water
hyacinth that tangle fishing gear, snag machinery, and have jammed the
mechanism of the city's floating bridge, a key commuter link into the
capital. A single-section barrier struggles to hold back that much plant
matter at once; the new two-part design is meant to keep processing the
river even during the worst of the bloom. Deployment is planned for early
2027.

It is not a dramatic story — no single dazzling number, no launch, no
paper. It is closer to the ordinary, necessary business of maintaining
infrastructure that works: watching six years of data reveal exactly where
a good design falls short, then rebuilding it for the specific problem
that's actually there, rather than the one it was designed for.
