title: Scientists catch a catalyst quietly changing jobs inside a clean-energy cell
kicker: Climate
author: The Editors
summary: The same silver nanoparticles turn out to do their most important work in different places depending on whether a solid oxide cell is making electricity or splitting water for hydrogen — a discovery that could make both processes more efficient.
order: 3
---

Solid oxide cells are two-way devices: run them one way and they generate
electricity, run them the other way and they split water into hydrogen, a
clean fuel. Researchers at Seoul National University, KAIST, and the Korea
Basic Science Institute have now shown that the silver nanocatalysts
inside these cells don't just do the same job twice — they relocate
depending on which job they're doing
([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/08/260810015714.htm)).

Using precisely controlled nanoparticle arrays on model electrodes, rather
than the tangled, hard-to-inspect electrode structures found in
commercial cells, the team led by professors WooChul Jung and Jeong Woo
Han, with KAIST's Sang Ouk Kim and KBSI's Beomgyun Jeong, tracked exactly
where silver catalyzes its reactions. During electricity generation, the
action happens at the interface between the catalyst and the electrode;
during hydrogen production, the catalyst's own surface becomes the main
reaction site. In head-to-head tests, silver outperformed cobalt,
palladium, and platinum at both jobs
([phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-08-silver-nanocatalysts-reaction-sites-power.html)).

The work was published August 10 in Energy & Environmental Science, where
it was selected for the outside back cover. Beyond solid oxide cells, the
team's method — precisely locating where a catalyst actually operates —
offers a general way to design better catalysts for other electrochemical
systems, with the near-term prize being distributed power generation that
runs more efficiently and green hydrogen, made by splitting water with
renewable electricity, that costs less energy to produce.
