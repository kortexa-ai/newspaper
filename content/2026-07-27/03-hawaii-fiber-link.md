Connecting six islands with a single reliable internet backbone has
turned out to be as much a negotiation with land, reef, and community as
it is an engineering problem. This week, the Hawaii Islands Fiber Link
(HIFL) project cleared a major one of those hurdles: the Department of
Hawaiian Home Lands approved the cable's path across beneficiary lands,
following a round of community consultation meetings
([Hawaii Tribune-Herald](https://www.hawaiitribune-herald.com/2026/07/27/hawaii-news/underwater-fiber-cable-project-clears-hurdle/)).

HIFL is designed to link Kauai, Oahu, Molokai, Lanai, Maui, and Hawaii
Island with a modern submarine fiber system, replacing aging inter-island
links with something built for how much data the state now moves.
Ocean Networks is contracted to install it, with the University of
Hawaii overseeing the project for the state and more than $400 million in
federal funding available to build it. Along the way, planners moved one
drilling site 1,500 feet farther offshore specifically to steer clear of
coral reefs — a small routing decision that matters over the 25-year
design life the cable is built for.

The project's completion date has slipped from 2026 to 2027, the direct
cost of taking permitting and community input seriously rather than
rushing past it. "Doing all the outreach and making sure folks understand
what we're doing, that's important," said Garret Yoshimi, who leads UH's
broadband projects. It's a smaller story than a satellite launch, but
island states live or die on connectivity, and a cable that respects the
reef it crosses is worth the extra year.
