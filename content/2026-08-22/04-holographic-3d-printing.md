---
title: A new printer builds hollow 3D shapes in one flash of light, not hours
kicker: Materials
author: The Editors
summary: Engineers at Utah and UT Austin can now print intricate parts with internal cavities running in every direction, cured all at once by a single patterned beam instead of built up layer by layer.
order: 4
---

Most 3D printing still works the way it always has: one layer at a time,
stacked up slowly until a shape emerges. Engineers at the University of
Utah and the University of Texas at Austin have built a printer that skips
the stacking almost entirely, curing an entire object in a single exposure
of patterned light — including objects with hollow cavities running through
them in every direction, something earlier one-shot methods couldn't
manage ([EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1140743)).

The trick is a nanoscale mask that shapes how laser light diffracts as it
passes through a photosensitive resin, creating precisely bright and dark
regions inside the liquid: bright where the object should solidify, dark
where it should stay liquid and wash away afterward. "We engineer the way
light flows through the resin such that there are bright regions where we
want curing to happen — the solid regions of the print — and darker regions
where we don't," said co-author Rajesh Menon. Earlier versions of this
holographic approach could only leave voids running in two dimensions;
this one can hollow out a shape along its length, width, and height at
once, all in a single shot that takes as little as 7.5 seconds, versus the
hours a layer-by-layer laser print of similar complexity would need.

The team, led by Menon and Dajun Lin at Utah alongside Michael Cullinan and
Zachariah Page at UT Austin, demonstrated the method on microtubule
structures as narrow as 6 micrometers with dimensional ratios up to 120 to
1 — thin, delicate lattice-like forms that would be slow and fragile to
build layer by layer. The work is published in Science Advances
([DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.aec3536](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aec3536)).
