title: Germany funds two years of work to close Flatpak's security gaps
kicker: Infrastructure
author: The Editors
summary: The German Sovereign Tech Agency is putting €508,640 into Flatpak's sandboxing, audio and networking permissions — public money aimed squarely at maintainer time rather than new ideas.
order: 4
---

Flatpak, the packaging format that lets Linux users install one app build
that works across different distributions inside a security sandbox, is
getting a €508,640 boost from Germany's Sovereign Tech Agency, announced
Thursday by the coordinating nonprofit Modal Collective
([Modal Collective](https://modal.cx/blog/announcing-flatpak-sta/)).
The money funds two years of contracted work running through the end of
2027.

The project has never lacked ideas for tightening Flatpak's sandbox —
it's lacked the maintainer hours to build them. This grant pays named
contributors, including Philip Withnall, Julian Sparber and Sam Hewitt
among others, to build new "portals": narrow, permission-gated doors that
let sandboxed apps request exactly the access they need and nothing more.
Planned work includes splitting microphone access from speaker output in
the audio portal, scope-based network isolation, a VPN portal modeled on
the permission systems in Android and iOS, and portals for spell-checking
and password autofill that currently require apps to bypass the sandbox
entirely
([Modal Collective](https://modal.cx/blog/announcing-flatpak-sta/)).

The announcement is candid about why this matters: "Flatpak is a mature
project and the best option that exists on GNU/Linux today, but its
security and sandboxing features still trail behind those of well-funded
proprietary platforms such as Android and iOS"
([Modal Collective](https://modal.cx/blog/announcing-flatpak-sta/)).
It's a rare case of public funding aimed not at a flashy new project but
at finishing the unglamorous plumbing an already-successful one has
needed for years.
