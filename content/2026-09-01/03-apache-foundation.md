The Apache Software Foundation released its annual report for fiscal year
2026 this week, and the headline number is a milestone the foundation
quietly passed late last year: it now counts 10,225 committers working
across 302 projects, having crossed the 10,000-committer mark for the
first time
([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/09/01/3354194/17401/en/the-apache-software-foundation-advances-open-source-infrastructure-and-celebrates-community-growth-in-fy26.html)).
Committers are volunteers who have earned the right to review and merge
code on Apache projects — the informal backbone of software like Kafka,
Spark, Cassandra and Airflow, much of which quietly runs inside banks,
telecoms and cloud providers most people have never heard of.

The report also details progress on Apache Trusted Releases, a tooling
initiative meant to modernize how the foundation's roughly 300 projects
ship software. Participating projects have spent the year testing
automated policy checks, release-vote management, and generation of
software bills of materials and cryptographic attestations — the kind of
paper trail that makes it easier to prove a package hasn't been tampered
with between a developer's laptop and a production server.

Alongside that, the foundation's Responsible AI Initiative, launched
earlier this year with a $10 million funding goal and seeded by
contributions including $1.5 million from Anthropic and $250,000 from
Alpha-Omega, continued building out governance practices and community
infrastructure for the open-source projects that increasingly sit
underneath AI and machine-learning systems
([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/09/01/3354194/17401/en/the-apache-software-foundation-advances-open-source-infrastructure-and-celebrates-community-growth-in-fy26.html)).
Taken together, the report reads less like a single announcement than a
ledger of an all-volunteer institution — nearly 27 years old now — still
adding both people and plumbing at a steady clip.
