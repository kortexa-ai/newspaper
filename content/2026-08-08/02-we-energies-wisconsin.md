title: Wisconsin breaks ground on enough clean power for 100,000 homes
kicker: Climate
author: The Editors
summary: We Energies and its partners started construction this week on a battery, wind, and solar buildout across four Wisconsin counties, adding to a state grid that's leaning harder on renewables to meet rising demand.

---

Three renewable energy projects moved from paperwork to construction
equipment in Wisconsin this week. We Energies announced that work is now
underway on the Darien Battery Storage facility in Walworth County (75
megawatts), the Dawn Harvest Solar and Battery Park in Rock County (150
megawatts of solar plus 50 megawatts of storage), and the Badger Hollow
Wind Energy Center spanning Iowa and Grant counties (110 megawatts)
([We Energies](https://news.we-energies.com/we-energies-launches-construction-on-solar-wind-and-battery-storage-projects/)).

Combined, the three projects are expected to generate enough electricity
to power roughly 100,000 homes and are supporting more than 450
construction jobs. All three had already cleared review by the Public
Service Commission of Wisconsin; Invenergy is developing and building
them, with We Energies as majority owner alongside Wisconsin Public
Service and Madison Gas and Electric.

"These renewable energy projects are part of our strategy to strengthen
reliability through a balanced mix of Wisconsin-based energy sources,"
said Mike Hooper, We Energies' president. Invenergy's senior vice
president of development, Daniel Litchfield, called the start of
construction "an important milestone for Wisconsin's energy future."

None of this is flashy. It's batteries in a field, turbines on a ridge,
panels on racking — the unglamorous machinery of a grid quietly adding
capacity it will need for a decade of rising demand. That's precisely
what makes it worth noting: 100,000 homes' worth of power, under
construction now, headed for a state that will use it.
