Amazon announced on Wednesday that its Prime Air drone-delivery service is
heading toward nearly 500 U.S. cities and towns by the end of 2026 — roughly
six times its current reach — with eleven new sites coming online first in
places like Tolleson, Arizona; Ruskin, Florida; Kansas City, Kansas;
Baton Rouge, Louisiana; Hazel Park and Pontiac, Michigan; and Richmond, San
Antonio, Richardson, and Waco, Texas. Launches in Georgia, Ohio, Illinois,
Idaho, and New York are described as coming soon after that
([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/19/amazons-prime-air-is-taking-off-in-nearly-500-u-s-cities/);
[Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/transportation/amazon-prime-air-drone-delivery-expansion)).

The service currently operates across seven states and has already delivered
hundreds of thousands of packages by air this year, according to Amazon.
Items weighing five pounds or less — about the size of a shoebox — can
arrive in as fast as 30 minutes, a speed unmatched by ground delivery
in most of the smaller cities and towns on the expansion list, many of
which are exactly the places that tend to wait longest for new logistics
infrastructure.

"Customers already turn to Amazon for fast Same- and Next-Day Delivery,
and Prime Air provides them an even speedier option when they need it,
with deliveries in as fast as 30 minutes," said David Carbon, Amazon's
vice president of Prime Air.

Each new site is expected to cover roughly 175 square miles, and the
program now flies under FAA Part 135 certification, the same authority
that governs commercial air-taxi operations. Prime Air has also expanded
internationally, with a site already running in Darlington, England. None
of this makes drone delivery ubiquitous overnight — nearly 500 locations
is still a small slice of the country — but a sixfold jump in a single
year, reaching well past the usual first-wave test markets and into
smaller cities and towns, is a genuine bet that the economics of small
aerial deliveries finally work outside a handful of flagship metros.
