---
title: US battery storage just had its biggest quarter on record
kicker: Climate
author: The Editors
summary: The country added 20.2 gigawatt-hours of grid battery capacity in three months, led by a single record-setting quarter in Arizona.
order: 2
---

The US grid added 20.2 gigawatt-hours of battery storage capacity in the
second quarter of 2026, the largest quarterly addition on record, according
to the latest US Energy Storage Market Outlook from the Solar Energy
Industries Association and Benchmark Mineral Intelligence
([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/08/31/the-us-added-a-record-20-2-gwh-of-battery-storage-in-just-3-months/)).
That brings total installations for the first half of 2026 to 30.8
gigawatt-hours, up 23 percent from the same period last year.

Utility-scale projects did most of the work, accounting for about 18
gigawatt-hours of the quarter's additions across seven separate
gigawatt-hour-scale projects in Arizona, California and Utah. Arizona alone
installed 6.2 gigawatt-hours, the strongest single-state quarter ever
recorded, ahead of Texas at 3.8 and California at 3.6. Just under half of
that utility-scale capacity was paired with solar generation, with the rest
built as standalone storage.

"Storage is a powerful reliability tool that strengthens our energy
security, meets rising demand, and puts downward pressure on electricity
bills," SEIA president and CEO Tim Pawlenty said of the report
([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/08/31/the-us-added-a-record-20-2-gwh-of-battery-storage-in-just-3-months/)).
Benchmark Mineral Intelligence now projects roughly 71 gigawatt-hours of
battery installations for all of 2026, about 20 percent more than last
year — and batteries have already supplied more electricity to the grid in
the first eight months of 2026 than they did in all of 2025, a sign of how
quickly storage is moving from a niche grid asset to a routine one.
