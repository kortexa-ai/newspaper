Sonoma State University powered on a 4.1-megawatt solar array with
1.55 megawatt-hours of battery storage on August 12, one of the largest
solar-plus-storage systems in Sonoma County. The panels sit as carports
over five campus parking lots, which means the project delivers shade
and shelter along with electricity — a detail that matters more than it
sounds, in a part of California that gets both fire season and
100-degree afternoons
([Solar Power World](https://www.solarpowerworldonline.com/2026/08/sonoma-state-university-powers-on-solar-carports-and-battery-storage/)).

TotalEnergies built and financed the system through a power purchase
agreement, meaning the university paid nothing upfront. The school
expects to save more than $5 million in utility costs over the first
five years, with the savings redirected into its Green Revolving Fund
to seed further efficiency projects. The array should generate upwards
of 7.9 million kilowatt-hours of clean power in its first year alone,
and it's microgrid-ready — able to island itself from the grid during
Public Safety Power Shutoffs, the preemptive outages California
utilities use to cut wildfire risk during high winds.

The project is part of a broader push: TotalEnergies says it now
supports more than 30 percent of the California State University
system's solar installations across 22 campuses, all tied to the CSU
system's commitment to reach net-zero carbon emissions by 2045. Faculty
and students will also use the live system as a working lab for
real-time clean energy data — which turns a parking lot upgrade into a
classroom, too.
