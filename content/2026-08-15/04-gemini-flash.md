title: Google's newest AI model got faster and cheaper in the same release

summary: Gemini 3.7 Flash arrived three weeks after its predecessor with meaningful coding gains and introductory pricing at half of what the last model launched at.

---

Google released Gemini 3.7 Flash on August 13, just three weeks after
Gemini 3.6 Flash, pitching it as the workhorse model for coding and
agent tasks rather than a flagship release. The gains are real by
Google's own benchmarks: on the DeepSWE v1.1 coding benchmark, the model
jumped from 49.0 percent to 65.3 percent, and on FrontierCode 1.1 it
improved from 34.4 to 43.6 percent. Document-processing performance
roughly doubled and business-automation task completion nearly
doubled as well
([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-3-7-flash/)).

What stands out is the pricing move alongside the performance jump.
Introductory rates — 75 cents per million input tokens and $3.75 per
million output tokens, in effect through the end of 2026 — are exactly
half of what Gemini 3.6 Flash launched at, before prices roughly double
again in January. Google says the underlying gain isn't just scale but
better reasoning under the hood: the model "adapts to roadblocks,
clarifies intent when needed, and follows instructions with greater
fidelity," according to the announcement, putting more effort into
multi-step planning before it acts.

The model is live now in Google AI Studio, Android Studio, and Google
Antigravity for developers, in the Gemini Enterprise platform for
businesses, and in Gemini Spark for subscribers in more than 160
countries. Three weeks between major model releases is a fast cadence
by any standard — a reminder of how quickly the baseline for "good
enough" keeps moving in this field, and, for once, in the direction of
cheaper rather than just bigger.
