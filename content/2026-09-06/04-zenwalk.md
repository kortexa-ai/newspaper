Somewhere behind the headline Linux distributions — Ubuntu, Fedora,
Debian and their well-funded corporate backers — a long tail of smaller
projects keeps going on volunteer time and stubbornness. Zenwalk is one
of them: a lightweight, Slackware-based distribution that's been around
for roughly two decades, without ever employing more than a handful of
people. On Friday, it shipped "Current Milestone 2026," its first major
release since Zenwalk 15.0 more than four years ago
([Zenwalk.org](https://www.zenwalk.org/2026/09/zenwalk-current-milestone-2026.html);
[Phoronix](https://www.phoronix.com/news/Zenwalk-2026-Current)).

The release pairs the latest upstream Slackware base with a Linux 7.1
kernel running the BORE scheduler, a community-developed tweak aimed
squarely at desktop responsiveness — the small, felt difference between a
system that stutters under load and one that doesn't. Xfce 4.20 is the
default desktop, Flatpak support is now built in rather than bolted on,
and the team rewrote its graphical system tools rather than letting them
quietly rot.

None of this will make anyone switch operating systems overnight, and
that's not really the point. Zenwalk has never chased market share; it
exists because a small group of people wanted a fast, unfussy Linux
distribution and were willing to keep building it long after the
attention moved elsewhere. Four years between releases is either a
worrying silence or a sign of a project taking its time to do the work
properly — this release, invisible to almost everyone outside a small
and loyal user base, argues for the latter.
