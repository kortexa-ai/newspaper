title: Two astronauts fixed the space station's video dish by hand, 250 miles up
kicker: Space
author: The Editors
summary: NASA's Anil Menon and ESA's Sophie Adenot completed a 6.5-hour spacewalk to install a replacement antenna, restoring the ISS's high-speed link to Houston.
order: 4
---

NASA astronaut Anil Menon and ESA astronaut Sophie Adenot spent six and a
half hours outside the International Space Station on Tuesday, finishing a
repair job they'd started the week before. Their target was a Space-to-Ground
antenna — the hardware that carries high-speed data, including video, between
Mission Control in Houston and the orbiting lab
([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/blogs/spacestation/2026/08/25/nasa-esa-spacewalkers-finish-installing-high-speed-antenna/)).

The original antenna had failed, and the two astronauts pulled it during a
spacewalk on August 18, stowing it temporarily on the station's exterior.
Tuesday's excursion, which ran from 8:27 a.m. to 2:57 p.m. EDT, was about
finishing the job: installing and connecting the spare unit, then securing
the failed one to a spare-parts platform on the station's truss for eventual
return or disposal. Initial checks after the swap showed good power and data
transmission, with ground controllers running overnight tests to confirm the
new antenna is fully operational
([Space.com](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/international-space-station/you-just-saved-the-video-dish-two-astronauts-complete-spacewalk-to-replace-iss-antenna)).

It was the 283rd spacewalk in the station's 25-year assembly and maintenance
history, the third of Menon's career and the second for Adenot. The work is
routine in the sense that astronauts have done thousands of hours of it
before — and remarkable in the sense that it never stops being two people in
pressurized suits, hand-swapping hardware in vacuum, to keep a laboratory the
size of a football field talking to the ground.
