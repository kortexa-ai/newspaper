Three separate construction sites across southern Wisconsin are now
underway on what utility We Energies says will add up to enough
generation and storage to power roughly 100,000 homes
([We Energies](https://news.we-energies.com/we-energies-launches-construction-on-solar-wind-and-battery-storage-projects/);
[Renewable Energy Magazine](https://www.renewableenergymagazine.com/panorama/we-energies-launches-construction-on-solar-wind-20260805)).

The three projects split across generation types: the Darien Battery
Storage facility in Walworth County, a 75-megawatt standalone battery
system; the Dawn Harvest Solar and Battery Park in Rock County,
combining 150 megawatts of solar with 50 megawatts of battery
storage; and the Badger Hollow Wind Energy Center, a 110-megawatt
wind farm spanning Iowa and Grant counties. Together they pair
generation with storage in a way that's increasingly the norm for new
utility-scale renewables — batteries that can bank power from sun and
wind and release it when the grid needs it most, rather than only
when the weather cooperates.

The company frames the build-out as a response to rising power
demand, and construction on all three sites is projected to support
more than 450 jobs. It's a useful reminder that the clean energy
transition shows up in ordinary places as ordinary economic activity —
union construction crews, county tax bases, and a grid getting more
resilient — rather than only in headline-grabbing announcements from
national labs or startups.

None of the three projects has a public completion date yet, and
"enough to power 100,000 homes" is a capacity figure, not a guarantee
of what the grid will draw at any given moment. But breaking ground on
nearly 300 combined megawatts of solar, wind, and storage in a single
state, in a single week, is the kind of unglamorous, cumulative
progress that adds up.
