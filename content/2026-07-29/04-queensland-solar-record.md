---
title: Queensland's grid set a solar record — in winter
kicker: Climate
author: The Editors
summary: Large-scale solar covered 38.2% of the state's electricity on a July morning, beating the previous record set in the more favorable month of April.
order: 4
---

Winter is solar power's off-season: shorter days and a lower sun angle
usually mean weaker output than the rest of the year. On the morning of
July 26, Queensland's grid didn't get that memo. At 9:10am, large-scale
solar farms supplied 38.2% of the state's electricity consumption,
breaking the previous record of 37.1% set on April 25 — a month with
considerably more daylight to work with
([RenewEconomy, citing analysis by Geoff Eldridge of Global Power
Energy](https://reneweconomy.com.au/sunshine-state-sets-new-solar-record-in-the-middle-of-winter/)).

At the moment of the record, utility-scale solar output reached
roughly 3,097 megawatts against total consumption of about 8,112
megawatts; output later peaked near 3.1 gigawatts that day, the state's
tenth-highest daily maximum on record. Battery storage had its own
milestone hours later: stored energy discharging back to the grid hit
5,646 megawatt-hours at 3:30pm, also a record. Combining solar, wind,
and storage, renewable output briefly covered close to 76% of the
state's consumption.

None of this means Queensland's grid runs on sunshine alone — coal and
gas still carry most winter demand outside these peak windows. But the
trend line is the story: a record that would have counted as remarkable
in peak summer sun happened instead in the season built to make it
hard, evidence that the state's fast-growing solar and battery buildout
is now large enough to set records outside its best conditions, not
just in them.
