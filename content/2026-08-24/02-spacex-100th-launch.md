At 12:25 a.m. Pacific on August 21, a Falcon 9 lifted off from Vandenberg
Space Force Base carrying 29 Starlink satellites, and with it SpaceX
crossed its 100th orbital launch of the year — a pace no launch provider
had reached before by late August
([Spaceflight Now](https://spaceflightnow.com/2026/08/21/live-coverage-spacex-to-launch-100th-orbital-mission-of-2026/)).

The booster that got it there, B1100, was flying its ninth mission, and it
landed itself on the droneship *Of Course I Still Love You* about eight and
a half minutes after liftoff — the 220th landing on that particular ship
and the 653rd successful Falcon booster landing overall. Elon Musk
confirmed the company now has more than 11,000 Starlink satellites in
orbit, the accumulated payload of a cadence that has turned an expendable
industry into one where the same hardware routinely flies, lands, and
flies again within days.

There's nothing dramatic in a Starlink mission on its own; it's the hundredth
one this year, following ninety-nine that mostly looked just like it, that
makes the number worth pausing on. Reusability was once a promise SpaceX
had to argue for. A tally like this is what it looks like once the argument
is over and the count just keeps climbing in the background.
