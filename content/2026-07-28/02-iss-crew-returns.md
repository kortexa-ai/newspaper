title: Home after 102 million miles
kicker: Space
author: The Editors
summary: NASA astronaut Chris Williams and two Roscosmos cosmonauts landed safely in Kazakhstan after 241 days aboard the International Space Station.
order: 2
---

Early Sunday morning, a Soyuz capsule carrying NASA astronaut Chris
Williams and Roscosmos cosmonauts Sergey Kud-Sverchkov and Sergei Mikaev
drifted down under parachutes southeast of Dzhezkazgan, Kazakhstan,
closing out a 241-day stay aboard the International Space Station
([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-astronaut-chris-williams-crewmates-return-from-space-station/);
[Space.com](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/human-spaceflight/nasa-astronaut-two-cosmonauts-return-to-earth-soyuz-ms-28)).
Touchdown came at 5:27 a.m. CDT, capping a mission that circled the
planet 3,856 times and covered more than 102 million miles.

The trio launched to the station on November 27, 2025 — a first
spaceflight for Williams and Mikaev, a second for Kud-Sverchkov. During
their stay, Williams completed two spacewalks, one preparing the station
for a power-system upgrade and another repairing a joint on the
Canadarm2 robotic arm, and worked on in-space manufacturing experiments
for high-performance computing and electronics components that are
easier to grow without gravity in the way.

"The space station helps NASA understand and overcome the challenges of
human spaceflight, expand commercial opportunities in low Earth orbit,
and build on the foundation for long-duration missions to the Moon...and
to Mars," NASA said in its release marking the crew's return. It's an
unglamorous kind of progress — no headline-grabbing breakthrough, just
another crew rotation completed safely after eight months of
maintenance, repair and research in orbit — but the quarter-century of
continuous human presence on the station rests entirely on missions like
this one going right, over and over, without exception.
