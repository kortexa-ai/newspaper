---
title: Engineers broke the one rule every semiconductor laser was built on
kicker: Science
author: The Editors
summary: University of Illinois researchers got a photonic-crystal laser to work without the perfectly repeating pattern the technology has always depended on, opening the door to lasers tuned like a dial instead of cast in a single mold.
order: 3
---

Photonic-crystal surface-emitting lasers have always relied on one strict
rule: the microscopic pattern etched into the chip has to repeat with
near-perfect regularity, or the light won't lase. Erin Raftery, a graduate
student at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign working with
professor Kent Choquette, just showed that rule isn't as strict as it
looked. Their quasi-periodic photonic-crystal surface-emitting laser swaps
the vertical holes of a conventional design for a buried dielectric layer —
silicon dioxide etched into a nonrepeating pattern, then sealed under grown
semiconductor — and it still lases at room temperature
([Applied Physics Letters](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0325678);
[Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-08-nonrepeating-photonic-crystal-enable-tunable.html)).

"We can have a nonperiodic pattern and more flexibility to tune it," Raftery
said. The payoff isn't a single faster laser; it's a more forgiving way of
building them. "You can only grow one kind of structure at a time, whereas
we can mix and match on the same substrate," Choquette said. "This could
allow us to build more reliable, better-performing lasers." That flexibility
matters for applications, in aerospace and defense sensing among them, that
need lasers tuned to different jobs without redesigning the chip from
scratch each time. The team is now working toward an electrically driven
version, the step that would take the idea from the lab bench to a device
you could actually plug in.
