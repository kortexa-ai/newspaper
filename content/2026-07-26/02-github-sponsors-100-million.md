title: Open source maintainers have now been paid $100 million through GitHub
kicker: Builders
author: The Editors
summary: GitHub Sponsors crossed $100 million in cumulative funding to maintainers this week — and the most recent $10 million arrived in five months, not the two years the first $10 million took.
order: 2
---

GitHub announced this week that its Sponsors program has funneled more
than $100 million to open source maintainers and projects since it
launched in 2019 — money moved directly from more than 280,000
individual developers and companies to the more than 70,000 maintainers
and organizations who write the code much of the internet quietly runs on
([GitHub Blog](https://github.blog/open-source/maintainers/100-million-for-open-source-a-milestone-built-by-the-community/)).

The pace is the real story. The first $10 million took nearly two years
to accumulate; the most recent $10 million took five months. Company
sponsorships — now roughly 40% of total funding, and worth on average
about 15 times an individual sponsorship — have helped drive that
acceleration, a sign that more organizations are treating the open
source they depend on as something worth paying into rather than simply
taking from.

For maintainers, the difference shows up as time. "GitHub Sponsors
changed how I think about open source... gave me a direct, sustainable
way to fund the time it takes," said developer Kelvin Tegelaar. Caleb
Porzio put it more plainly: "Sponsorships allowed me to quit my job and
work on them full time."

GitHub is careful not to oversell the milestone — its own post notes
that funding gaps and maintainer burnout haven't gone away. But a
hundred million dollars that didn't exist for open source work seven
years ago now does, and it's arriving faster every year.
