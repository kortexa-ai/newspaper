title: Dark matter might push on itself — just not the way anyone guessed

summary: A new theoretical study finds that giving dark matter an extra force doesn't speed up galaxy formation, as expected, but slows it down instead.

Nearly everything astronomers know about dark matter comes from
watching what it does to gravity, since it doesn't emit, absorb, or
reflect light. That leaves plenty of room for the possibility that
dark matter particles interact with each other through some force
beyond gravity too faint to have been noticed yet — and physicists
have spent years asking what such a force would actually do to the
universe if it existed.

Zachary Weiner of the Perimeter Institute for Theoretical Physics, with
Marco Costa, Cyril Creque-Sarbinowski, and Olivier Simon, worked
through the math in detail and published the result in the *Journal
of Cosmology and Astroparticle Physics*
([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/08/260801042822.htm)).
Their starting assumption seemed like a safe bet: an added attractive
force between dark matter particles should help them clump together
faster, accelerating the growth of galaxies and the cosmic web that
holds them. Instead, they found the opposite. The same force that
pulls dark matter particles together also causes them to lose
effective mass as the universe expands, which weakens their overall
gravitational pull — and that weakening effect wins out, net
suppressing structure growth rather than speeding it up.

"The first thing you would expect is that giving dark matter an
additional attractive force should make structures grow faster," Weiner
said. "But another effect comes into play at the same time." It's a
reminder of how little is settled about dark matter's basic nature,
and a useful one: the prediction gives observers a concrete, testable
signature — slower-than-expected structure growth — to look for in the
next generation of galaxy surveys. "The Universe is often more subtle
than our intuition," Weiner added. "That's exactly why we have to keep
testing these ideas."
