---
title: A Starship finally came back in one piece
kicker: Space
author: The Editors
summary: SpaceX pulled its 13th Starship test vehicle intact from the Indian Ocean, the first to survive a full flight and splashdown, and is shipping it home for engineers to study.
order: 2
---

Every previous Starship test flight ended the same way: the vehicle broke
apart, burned up, or was deliberately destroyed somewhere over an ocean.
The one that launched from Starbase, Texas on July 24 didn't. It flew a
full, hour-long, space-skimming trajectory, survived re-entry, and
splashed down in the Indian Ocean still in one piece — floating, intact,
recoverable
([ABC News](https://abcnews.com/Technology/wireStory/spacex-recovers-starship-indian-ocean-month-after-launch-136036203)).

A month later, SpaceX went and got it. Crews pulled the vehicle from the
water near Christmas Island in what the company is calling its biggest
salvage operation to date, and it's now on a slow boat back to Texas — a
trip expected to take several months
([Norfolk Daily News](https://norfolkdailynews.com/news/national/news_national/spacex-recovers-starship-from-indian-ocean-a-month-after-launch/article_8d24832e-a91d-4e58-b74b-e3bb08edb905.html)).
"Congratulations to the entire SpaceX Recovery team for battling through
challenging conditions to give our engineers access to a wealth of data to
continue rapidly developing Starship," the company said.

That data is the point. A vehicle that survives its flight can be
inspected in ways a debris field can't be: heat-shield tiles checked
individually, structural stress mapped, the gap between what engineers
predicted and what actually happened measured directly rather than
inferred from telemetry and wreckage. It's the unglamorous, incremental
work that reusable rockets are actually built on.

Starship remains central to two much bigger ambitions — NASA's Artemis
lunar landings, targeted for 2028, and Elon Musk's long-stated goal of a
self-sustaining city on Mars
([ABC News](https://abcnews.com/Technology/wireStory/spacex-recovers-starship-indian-ocean-month-after-launch-136036203)).
Thirteen test flights in, this is the first time a full vehicle has come
back whole enough to teach its builders something new by simply existing.
