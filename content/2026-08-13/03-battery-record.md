Records in grid-scale batteries tend to fall fast these days, and
Australia's National Electricity Market keeps proving it. At 6:25 p.m. on
August 11, battery output across the NEM hit 4,325 megawatts — a new
all-time high, and one that beat the previous record of 4,131 megawatts
set just eleven days earlier, on July 31. In that same interval,
batteries supplied 14.42 percent of the entire market's electricity
demand, itself a record
([Energy-Storage.News](https://www.energy-storage.news/australia-battery-storage-systems-hit-record-4325mw-discharge-as-nem-cycling-swing-reaches-8-3gw/)).

The pattern held at the state level, too: New South Wales batteries
discharged 1,776 megawatts at 6 p.m., covering nearly 17 percent of the
state's load, both state records, while Victorian batteries had set a
separate charging record earlier that afternoon, pulling in 1,383.6
megawatts of cheap midday solar to bank for the evening peak
([Energy-Storage.News](https://www.energy-storage.news/australia-battery-storage-systems-hit-record-4325mw-discharge-as-nem-cycling-swing-reaches-8-3gw/)).

That charge-in-the-day, discharge-at-dusk rhythm is exactly the job grid
batteries are meant to do — smoothing out the gap between when solar
panels produce and when households actually need the power. Australia's
market operator reports 9.1 gigawatts of new generation and storage
connected at full output in the past financial year, more than double the
year before, with battery projects leading the mix
([Energy-Storage.News](https://www.energy-storage.news/australia-adds-9-1gw-of-new-generation-and-storage-in-fy26-with-battery-storage-dominating/)).
Each broken record is a small one. Together they are how a grid quietly
changes what it runs on.
