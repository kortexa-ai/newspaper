---
title: The AI industry's two agent standards now share one roof
kicker: Open Source
author: The Editors
summary: Google handed its Agent2Agent protocol to the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation, putting it under the same neutral governance as Anthropic's Model Context Protocol.
order: 2
---

Software standards usually splinter before they consolidate. This month
one of AI's went the other way. On August 20, Google's Agent2Agent (A2A)
protocol formally became a hosted project of the Agentic AI Foundation
(AAIF), the Linux Foundation body that already stewards Anthropic's Model
Context Protocol (MCP)
([Linux Foundation](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/a2a-protocol-surpasses-150-organizations-lands-in-major-cloud-platforms-and-sees-enterprise-production-use-in-first-year),
[Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/17/a2a-agentic-ai-foundation-open-ai-standards)).
The two protocols solve different problems — MCP lets an individual AI
agent plug into tools and data sources, while A2A lets separate agents,
often built by different companies, talk to and delegate work to each
other — but neither is much use if only one vendor controls it.

Google announced A2A in April 2025 and donated it to the Linux Foundation
that June, a hedge against exactly the outcome AAIF's formation avoids:
one company's agent-to-agent protocol becoming the industry standard by
default while remaining that company's to change unilaterally. Since
launching in December, AAIF has grown from fewer than forty member
organizations to more than 250, with Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Anthropic,
OpenAI, Bloomberg, Cloudflare, Shopify, and Block all backing the same
governance structure
([Linux Foundation](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/a2a-protocol-surpasses-150-organizations-lands-in-major-cloud-platforms-and-sees-enterprise-production-use-in-first-year)).
A2A itself has picked up more than 150 contributing organizations and
reached production use inside major cloud platforms in its first year
under open governance.

The practical upshot is narrower than the announcement makes it sound:
this is plumbing, not a product launch. But plumbing is exactly what an
industry needs before "AI agents from different companies coordinating
with each other" becomes something builders can rely on rather than
something that happens to work today and might not tomorrow. Having the
two protocols that define how agents talk to tools and to one another
sit under the same neutral roof, rather than in competing corporate
silos, is the kind of unglamorous groundwork that tends to matter more
in three years than it does this week.
