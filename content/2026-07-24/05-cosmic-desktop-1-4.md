COSMIC, the Rust-built desktop environment System76 has been developing
in the open as an alternative to GNOME and KDE, got its 1.4 release on
July 22 — another round of the incremental polish that's turned it from a
curiosity into a genuinely usable daily driver
([Phoronix](https://www.phoronix.com/news/COSMIC-Epoch-1.4), corroborated
by [Linuxiac](https://linuxiac.com/cosmic-desktop-1-4-improves-system-monitor-with-app-controls-and-gpu-stats/)
and [Tux Machines](https://news.tuxmachines.org/n/2026/07/23/Free_and_Open_Source_Software_howtos_and_Installations.shtml)).

The changes are small individually and add up collectively. The
compositor gets better input and window handling, along with more
accurate screen-edge pointer tracking on displays using fractional
scaling — a longstanding sore spot for Linux desktops in general. The
panel picks up crash fixes. COSMIC's system monitor, still one of the
project's newer pieces, adds proper quit and force-quit controls for
misbehaving apps, along with GPU frequency tracking and the ability to
switch its graphs between CPU and GPU views. The release also bundles a
new default sound theme and a fresh batch of translation updates.

None of this is headline-grabbing on its own, and that's rather the
point. COSMIC is still a young desktop environment competing against two
that have had over two decades to work out their rough edges. Shipping
steady, unglamorous point releases — the kind that fix a crash here and a
scaling bug there — is how an open-source project earns the right to be
someone's daily desktop instead of just an interesting experiment they
tried once.
