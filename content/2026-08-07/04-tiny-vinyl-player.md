Vinyl's revival has mostly meant bigger: heavier pressings, deluxe box
sets, more plastic per album than the format ever used in its first
life. Tiny Vinyl has been quietly building the opposite — four-inch
records, a fraction the size of a standard 12-inch LP, each one
traditionally pressed with a single song per side and made from what the
company calls 100 percent bio-attributed vinyl. A disc uses roughly a
tenth of the material of a full-size record, and because they're cut for
manual stylus placement, they already played on any ordinary turntable.

This week the company launched the accessory that turns the format into
its own small ecosystem: the Tiny Vinyl Player, a $49.99 belt-drive
turntable built specifically for the four-inch discs, with built-in
speakers, Bluetooth, and a suitcase-style leatherette shell about the
size of a lunchbox
([New Atlas](https://newatlas.com/home-entertainment/tiny-vinyl-player-miniature-turntable)).
It's a niche product solving a niche problem — nobody strictly needed a
dedicated player for records that already worked on the turntables people
already own. But it's also a small, complete bet that a lower-material,
lower-cost version of a beloved format can stand on its own rather than
riding along as a novelty, and that's a bet worth rooting for on a
Friday morning.
