---
title: A fusion startup cut its fuel-making time from a week to two hours
kicker: Climate
author: The Editors
summary: Inertia Enterprises found a way to freeze its hydrogen fuel pellets far faster than the national lab process it grew out of, a step toward fusion plants that don't need huge tritium stockpiles.
order: 3
---

Fusion power plants built around inertial confinement — the approach that
achieved ignition at the National Ignition Facility in 2022 — need a steady
supply of tiny pellets of frozen hydrogen fuel, precisely layered and
smooth enough to survive a laser blast. Making one has historically taken
close to a week. Inertia Enterprises, a startup spun out of that same lab
work, says it has cut that down to two or three hours, with a path to
under one
([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/20/inertia-enterprises-finds-a-way-to-make-its-fusion-fuel-fast/)).

The company was co-founded by Annie Kritcher, who led the physics design
behind the National Ignition Facility's 2022 ignition breakthrough, and Jeff
Lawson, the former Twilio chief executive; a first-of-its-kind arrangement
under the CHIPS and Science Act let Kritcher help start the company while
keeping her post at Lawrence Livermore, where Inertia holds access to
roughly 200 related patents through an April 2026 agreement with the lab
([Heatmap News](https://heatmap.news/climate-tech/inertia-livermore-fusion)).

The speedup matters because tritium, one of the two hydrogen isotopes in
the fuel, is radioactive and costs around $30,000 a gram. A plant that
takes a week to make each pellet has to keep a large, expensive tritium
inventory on hand just to keep running; one that can turn pellets out in
hours needs far less. Inertia's commercial design aims for ten pellets a
second. "I put on my commercial hat and was like, wait a minute, I know the
word for this: prototypes," CEO Jeff Lawson said of realizing the lab's
careful, one-at-a-time process could be industrialized
([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/20/inertia-enterprises-finds-a-way-to-make-its-fusion-fuel-fast/)).
It's one of ten milestones the company has set for itself on the way to a
pilot plant it targets for sometime in the 2030s — a small, unglamorous
fix, in a field where the unglamorous fixes are what turn a physics
demonstration into a power plant.
