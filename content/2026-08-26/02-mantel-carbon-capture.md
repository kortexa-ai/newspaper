title: An MIT spinout raises fresh cash to bring carbon capture out of the lab

summary: Mantel, whose molten-salt system captures CO2 from smokestacks while recovering the waste heat, closed an $18 million round to push its technology into heavy industry.

Carbon capture has long had a cost problem: the equipment that scrubs CO2
from a smokestack usually eats a large share of the plant's own energy to do
it. Mantel, a Cambridge, Massachusetts startup spun out of MIT's Department
of Chemical Engineering in 2022, has been building around a different idea —
a loop of molten borate salt that captures CO2 at high temperature and
recovers the heat as usable steam rather than throwing it away. The company
says the approach cuts energy losses by 97% compared with conventional
systems and runs at under half the industry's typical cost per ton
([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/08/24/3349835/0/en/mantel-raises-strategic-investment-from-constellation-technology-ventures-and-azimut-to-scale-commercial-carbon-capture-deployment.html)).

On Monday, Mantel announced an $18 million strategic investment from
Constellation Technology Ventures and Azimut Investments, bringing its total
raised to $50 million
([ESG Today](https://www.esgtoday.com/mantel-raises-18-million-to-commercialize-carbon-capture-solution-for-heavy-industry/)).
The money is earmarked for moving out of pilot projects and into commercial
deployments: one already underway with a major Canadian oil and gas
producer, another at Kruger's Wayagamack paper mill in Québec, and a planned
installation at the 1.6-gigawatt TerraSpark Energy Campus in West Virginia.

"This moment is about execution and how fast we can build... at the speed
this transition demands," said co-founder and CEO Cameron Halliday. Carbon
capture remains a contested tool in the climate fight — critics note it can
extend the life of fossil-fuel infrastructure — but for industries like
pulp, paper, and oil and gas that are hard to electrify outright, a cheaper
way to keep their CO2 out of the atmosphere is progress worth having.
