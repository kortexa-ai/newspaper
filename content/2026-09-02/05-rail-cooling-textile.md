---
title: South Korea cools its rails with a magnetic blanket instead of a hose
kicker: Infrastructure
author: The Editors
summary: A detachable, reflective textile that clips onto rails with magnets cut summer track temperatures by nearly 11 degrees Celsius in field trials, without the water and cost of spray cooling.
order: 5
---

Hot summers warp rails, and the standard fix — spraying tracks with water
— is expensive and consumes a resource many regions can't spare. The
Korea Railroad Research Institute (KRRI) has been testing an alternative
that needs neither water nor ongoing labor: a multilayer textile,
reflecting more than 85 percent of solar radiation, that clips onto the
rail with magnets and lifts off again just as easily
([techxplore](https://techxplore.com/news/2026-09-cooling-overheated-summer-railways-thermal.html)).

In field demonstrations on the conventional Gwangju Line in summer 2024
and on the high-speed Jungbu Inland Line from June through October 2025,
the textile cut peak rail temperatures by as much as 10.9°C. Durability
testing found no performance loss after simulating more than a decade of
use, and the magnetic attachment held fast against simulated winds of 66
meters per second — about 1.5 times stronger than the gusts a train
generates at 300 kilometers per hour. "This technology goes beyond simply
lowering rail temperatures — it presents a paradigm for protecting
railway safety," said chief researcher Kang Dong-hoon, who led the work.
The technology has since earned South Korea's National New Excellent
Technology certification and a technology-transfer agreement with
PITCHCABLE Co., putting a low-cost, water-free way to protect rail
infrastructure from heat on a path to wider use.
