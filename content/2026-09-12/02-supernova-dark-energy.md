title: The biggest catalog of exploding stars ever built says dark energy might not be constant
kicker: Science
author: The Editors
summary: A University of Queensland-led effort unified three decades of supernova surveys into one dataset of 2,884 exploding stars, adding fresh evidence that the force pushing the universe apart is changing over time.
order: 2
---

For nearly 30 years, cosmologists have treated dark energy — the mysterious force accelerating the universe's expansion — as a fixed constant, the same yesterday as it will be a billion years from now. A new dataset assembled by researchers at the University of Queensland is the latest and largest piece of evidence suggesting that assumption might be wrong ([UQ News](https://news.uq.edu.au/2026-09-big-supernova-dataset-challenges-dark-energy-theory); [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-09-big-supernova-dataset-dark-energy.html)).

The team combined observations of 2,884 Type Ia supernovae — the reliable "standard candle" explosions astronomers use to measure cosmic distances — pulled from multiple surveys spanning three decades into a single, internally consistent framework, in a paper posted September 4 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05053)). "We've rebuilt three decades of astronomical observations into a single, consistent framework," said Ryan Camilleri, the PhD candidate who led the work.

The result strengthens a pattern that has been quietly accumulating for a couple of years now: hints, from more than one independent method, that dark energy's strength may be weakening as the universe ages rather than staying fixed. "Two completely independent measurements have found hints of time variation in dark energy, challenging the standard model," said UQ astrophysicist Tamara Davis, a co-author.

Nobody is rewriting cosmology textbooks yet — "hints" is still the operative word, and extraordinary claims about the fundamental forces of the universe demand correspondingly extraordinary evidence. But a unified catalog this size, cross-checked against independent surveys like the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument, gives future telescopes a firmer foundation to test the idea against. Understanding whether dark energy is fixed or evolving is, in a real sense, understanding the ultimate fate of everything — and this catalog is a more solid floor to stand on while asking that question.
