---
title: India opens its first quantum chip foundry, with 10,000 qubits in its sights
kicker: Infrastructure
author: The Editors
summary: Bengaluru startup QpiAI inaugurated an eight-inch quantum processing unit fabrication facility, betting that quantum computing's biggest bottleneck is manufacturing, not physics.
order: 2
---

QpiAI has opened an eight-inch quantum processing unit foundry in Jakkur,
Bengaluru, the second phase of a 70,000-square-foot R&D campus built around
Class 100 and Class 1,000 cleanrooms. The facility can already fabricate
flip-chip superconducting processors carrying up to 128 physical qubits, and
the company says a third phase, due in 2027, will push that toward 10,000
([The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/08/17/qpiai-opens-quantum-chip-foundry-in-india-targets-10000-qubit-processors/);
[Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/qpiai-inaugurates-8-inch-quantum-chip-foundry-in-bengaluru-targeting-10000-qubit-qpus/)).

"Quantum computing today is being held back because there is not enough
innovation in manufacturing," said QpiAI chief executive Nagendra Nagaraja.
It's an unglamorous argument, but a persuasive one: the company has already
built four processors in-house, from the 8-qubit QVidya to the 64-qubit
Kaveri, and argues that owning fabrication end-to-end, rather than depending
on a handful of foreign foundries, is what actually lets a quantum hardware
company iterate. QpiAI has put $20-25 million into the facility so far, with
another $10-15 million planned, backed in part by a $32 million Series A
round and India's National Quantum Mission. It still imports about a fifth
of its raw materials, with a plan to localize that supply chain as capacity
grows, and eventually to pair the chips with AI clusters in what it calls
Quantum Supremacy Centres aimed at pharmaceutical simulation, logistics, and
materials science.
