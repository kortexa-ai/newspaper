title: The world's biggest fusion reactor just got its last major piece
kicker: Energy
author: The Editors
summary: With the ninth and final vacuum vessel sector now assembled in France, ITER's 5,000-ton donut-shaped heart — the vessel meant to hold plasma hotter than the sun — is structurally complete.
order: 2
---

Building a fusion reactor means building a container for something
that doesn't want to be contained: plasma heated past 100 million
degrees Celsius, hot enough to vaporize any material it touches,
suspended instead in an ultra-high vacuum by magnetic fields alone.
That container just came together. At a "Korea-ITER Fusion Day" event
in Cadarache, southern France, on July 22, engineers completed
assembly of the ninth and final vacuum vessel sector module and moved
it into the Tokamak pit to join the other eight, forming the complete
donut-shaped vessel — roughly 5,000 tons of steel that will encase
ITER's plasma once the reactor is operating
([PR Newswire via Manila Times](https://www.manilatimes.net/2026/07/23/tmt-newswire/pr-newswire/hd-hyundai-heavy-industries-reaches-major-milestone-in-iter-nuclear-fusion-project/2389859);
corroborated by [Taiwan News](https://www.taiwannews.com.tw/news/6406441)
and [Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/energy/world-largest-fusion-reactor-hd-hyundai-module)).

South Korea's HD Hyundai Heavy Industries built four of the nine
sectors — two under a contract awarded in 2010, two more picked up in
2016 after being reassigned from the European Union — and finished
delivering all four back in 2024. Each sector stands 11.3 meters tall,
spans 6.6 meters, and weighs about 400 tons on its own, built to
withstand the magnetic and thermal stresses of a working tokamak
without deforming. "Successful fabrication and installation represents
a major milestone demonstrating technological competitiveness of
Korean industry," said Won Kwang-shik, senior executive vice president
at HD Hyundai Heavy Industries.

ITER — the International Thermonuclear Experimental Reactor — is a
35-nation collaboration built to prove that fusion can produce more
energy than it consumes at power-plant scale, something no reactor has
yet demonstrated for sustained periods. The project has taken decades
and cost tens of billions of dollars, and a complete vacuum vessel
doesn't mean a working reactor: magnets, cooling systems, and years of
integration and testing remain before ITER attempts its first plasma.
But the vessel is the part everything else has to fit inside, and for
a machine this large, having its central structure finished and
in place is the difference between a blueprint and a building.
