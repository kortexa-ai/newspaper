---
title: The free screen reader that millions rely on just got a magnifier
kicker: Accessibility
author: The Editors
summary: NVDA 2026.2 adds a built-in screen magnifier, touch navigation for the web, and a handful of small fixes that add up to a more independent day for low-vision users.
order: 4
---

NVDA, the free and open-source screen reader for Windows built by NV
Access, has shipped version 2026.2, and the headline feature is one that
widens who the tool serves: a built-in Magnifier, offering zoom and color
filtering for users with low vision rather than only those who are fully
blind
([NV Access](https://www.nvaccess.org/post/nvda-2026-2/)). It currently
works in fullscreen mode, with docked modes — magnifying just part of the
screen while the rest stays put — planned for a future release.

The rest of the update is the kind of unglamorous polish that screen-reader
users notice every single day. Touch support has expanded: people
navigating web pages on a touchscreen can now flick between links,
headings, and form fields the way keyboard users tab between them, instead
of hunting by feel. Speech gets a new repeat command (NVDA+X) for catching
something read too quickly, plus support for custom speech dictionaries
from add-ons. Braille users get automatic scrolling on supported displays
and multi-button gesture support for DotPad devices, and Liblouis, the
open-source braille translation engine NVDA relies on, picks up new Italian
and Estonian tables. There's even a small mercy for the cautious: an Undo
button now appears when you reset NVDA to factory defaults.

None of this is a single dramatic leap. It's a free tool, maintained by a
small nonprofit, adding a magnifier here and a gesture there because the
people who use it every day asked for exactly these things — and a
magnifier that didn't exist last month means one more group of low-vision
users can now get real use out of it without buying anything else.
