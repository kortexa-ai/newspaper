---
title: Britain makes it legal to just plug a solar panel into the wall
kicker: Climate
author: The Editors
summary: New rules that took effect August 27 let households across Great Britain buy and self-install small solar systems without a certified installer or planning permission.
order: 2
---

As of August 27, anyone in England, Scotland or Wales can buy a small solar
setup, plug it into a spare socket, and start generating their own power —
no certified installer, and in England, no planning permission required
([pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/08/27/plug-in-solar-panels-now-legal-in-the-uk/)).

The change, set out in a new statutory instrument and an amendment to the
UK's wiring regulations, caps self-installed systems at 800 watts — enough,
the government estimates, to cover about a fifth of an average home's
electricity use and worth up to £110 a year in savings
([pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/08/27/plug-in-solar-panels-now-legal-in-the-uk/)).
Plug-in solar has been common for years in Germany and the Netherlands,
where balcony-mounted panels are a familiar sight; Britain's building and
electrical codes had simply never caught up. Now they have.

"This is about giving people access to cheap, clean power that they
haven't been able to access before," Solar Energy UK chief executive Chris
Hewett said of the change
([pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/08/27/plug-in-solar-panels-now-legal-in-the-uk/)).
Retailers moved fast: Argos said it was "really pleased" to start selling
compliant kits, and the government's own product database listed its first
approved devices within days of the rule taking effect
([pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/08/27/plug-in-solar-panels-now-legal-in-the-uk/)).

The list of approved hardware is still short — only a handful of devices
had cleared the new specification as the rule took effect — and plug-in
battery storage remains excluded pending a separate safety review. But for
renters and flat-dwellers long shut out of rooftop solar because they
don't own the roof, this is the first time UK law has offered a legal,
literal way in: a panel, a cable, and an ordinary wall socket.
