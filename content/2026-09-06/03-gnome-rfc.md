---
title: GNOME is writing down how it makes big decisions
kicker: Open Source
author: The Editors
summary: The desktop project has opened the final comment period on a formal RFC process, giving major design and governance choices a documented paper trail for the first time.
order: 3
---

Open-source projects make plenty of their biggest calls the same informal
way they always have: a mailing-list thread, a merge request, a
maintainer's judgment call, and then the decision just becomes how things
work — with no record of what alternatives were weighed or why one won.
GNOME is trying to fix that for itself. On Thursday, contributor Sophie
Herold announced the final comment period for a new, formal Request for
Comments process, meant to govern how the project makes its major
architectural and governance decisions going forward
([GNOME blog](https://blogs.gnome.org/sophieh/2026/09/04/introduction-of-gnome-rfc-process-start-of-final-comment-period/)).

The mechanics are borrowed from a pattern that's worked well elsewhere in
open source, from Rust to Python: a written proposal, a public record of
the alternatives considered, and a defined comment window — 14 days by
default, extended to a full month for this inaugural round, running
through October 4. Any active GNOME Foundation member gets a say before
it's adopted.

It's an unglamorous kind of progress — no new feature ships because of
it, no user will notice a pixel move — but it's the sort of infrastructure
that determines how well a decades-old project can still make hard
decisions without splintering. GNOME has weathered its share of
governance disputes over the years; a documented process for resolving
the next one, before it starts, is exactly the kind of quiet
institution-building that rarely gets a headline and rarely gets credit,
except from the people who'll be glad it exists the next time the project
needs to change its mind about something big.
