---
title: Captioning got a lot cheaper this week
kicker: AI
author: The Editors
summary: Microsoft's new transcription model prices audio at ten cents an hour, a drop that quietly widens who can afford accurate captions, transcripts, and accessibility tooling.
order: 3
---

Turning speech into accurate text has quietly been one of AI's more useful
day-to-day jobs — powering captions, meeting notes, and transcripts for
anyone who can't or doesn't want to listen to raw audio. On Wednesday,
Microsoft AI released MAI-Transcribe-2, a new speech-recognition model, and
priced it at $0.10 per hour of audio through the end of the year — well
below what comparable services have charged
([Microsoft AI](https://microsoft.ai/news/mai-transcribe-2-is-the-fastest-most-accurate-and-cheapest-speech-recognition-model-in-the-world/)).

The model itself is solid rather than flashy: it tops the FLEURS benchmark
across 60 languages with a 5.2% average word-error rate, handles
speaker diarization and word-level timestamps, and can follow conversations
that switch languages mid-sentence — the kind of code-switching common in
Hinglish or Spanglish speech that trips up many transcription systems.
Microsoft points to accessibility services and closed captioning
specifically among its intended uses, alongside legal and clinical
note-taking.

The number worth sitting with is the price. Accurate, multilingual
transcription has been a real cost for the small captioning shops,
nonprofits, and independent podcasters and educators who need it most —
often the ones with the thinnest budgets for accessibility work in the
first place. A dramatically cheaper hour of transcription doesn't change what
the technology can do, but it changes who can afford to use it well, and
that's the more durable kind of progress: not a new capability, but an old
one finally within reach for more people. The model is available now
through Microsoft Foundry and the MAI Playground.
