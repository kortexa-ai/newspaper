Long road trips have been the stubborn holdout for electric vehicles — fine
around town, trickier when the nearest fast charger might be an hour out of
the way. That gap is closing. The charging network built jointly by travel
center chain Pilot, General Motors, and charging network EVgo has passed
300 locations and 1,300 fast-charging stalls spread across 40 states, now
reaching an estimated 75 percent of the contiguous United States by area —
more than 2.2 million square miles
([Pilot Company](https://newsroom.pilotcompany.com/pilot-general-motors-and-evgo-power-ev-road-trips-with-more-than-300-fast-charging-locations-nationwide)).

The network, launched in 2022, places chargers at Pilot and Flying J travel
centers along interstate corridors rather than scattered through city
centers, aiming squarely at the highway road-trip use case rather than
daily commuting. Stations typically add 24-hour staffing, free Wi-Fi, and
on-site retail — the same amenities that have made truck stops a fixture of
American driving for decades, now extended to drivers waiting on a charge.
Drivers have rated the network 9.41 out of 10 on Plugshare, the
crowdsourced charger-reliability app, as of this July.

The partners are now more than halfway to their stated goal of 2,000 stalls
across 500 locations. It's not a technological leap — it's steady,
unglamorous infrastructure building, one interstate exit at a time. But
that is exactly the kind of progress that turns "electric vehicles are
improving" into "electric vehicles work for the trip I'm actually taking."
