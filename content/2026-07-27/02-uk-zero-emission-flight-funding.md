---
title: Britain puts £7.3 million behind electric and hydrogen planes
kicker: Climate
author: The Editors
summary: Eight British companies just got government funding to fly zero-emission aircraft, from electric air taxis to hydrogen storage systems.
order: 2
---

The UK government announced on July 23 that it is putting £7.3 million
behind eight companies racing to get electric and hydrogen-powered
aircraft off the ground, part of a broader £43 million push toward
zero-emission flight
([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/over-7-million-to-fuel-zero-emission-flight-across-the-uk)).

The money is split across genuinely different bets on how flight
decarbonizes. Beta Technologies and Vertical Aerospace are sharing up to
£3.2 million to keep developing electric aircraft — Vertical's six-seat
design is aiming for passenger services by 2029. Other grants go toward
hydrogen storage systems and electric charging infrastructure built
specifically for airports, the unglamorous plumbing that any zero-emission
fleet needs before it can fly a real schedule. Rolls-Royce, EasyJet, and
Exeter Airport are separately running hydrogen research programs feeding
into the same goal.

"The next generation of flight is taking off in the UK," said Transport
Secretary Heidi Alexander. "By powering up electric and hydrogen-powered
planes, we're bringing passengers closer to the flights of the future."

None of this replaces a long-haul jet next year, or even in five years.
But it's a useful reminder that decarbonizing aviation is less one
breakthrough than a hundred smaller ones — batteries, hydrogen tanks,
charging plugs, airport ground equipment — funded steadily enough that
some of them stick. The UK's aerospace sector contributes roughly £20
billion a year to its economy; this is a government betting a sliver of
that on the companies trying to make its next chapter cleaner, with a
2050 net-zero aviation target as the deadline they're all working
against.
