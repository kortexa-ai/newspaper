title: A South African telescope hears the faintest signal yet from the young universe
kicker: Space
author: The Editors
summary: Astronomers used the MeerKAT array to directly detect hydrogen gas from billions of years ago, validating a technique that could map the cosmos far faster than counting galaxies one by one.
order: 2
---

Astronomers using South Africa's MeerKAT radio telescope have directly
detected an extraordinarily faint radio signal from neutral hydrogen gas
that dates back four to five billion years, a result that opens a new,
much faster way to map the large-scale structure of the universe
([University of Manchester](https://www.manchester.ac.uk/about/news/astronomers-use-meerkat-to-directly-detect-faint-hydrogen-signal-from-the-distant-universe)).

Hydrogen naturally emits at a wavelength of 21 centimeters, and as the
universe expands, that signal stretches further the older and more
distant its source. Rather than picking out individual galaxies one at a
time, the team — led by Dr. Sourabh Paul of the University of Manchester
and University of the Western Cape, alongside Professor Mário Santos, Dr.
Laura Wolz and Dr. Zhaoting Chen — used a technique called hydrogen
intensity mapping, which reads the combined glow of countless unresolved
galaxies at once. What makes this detection notable is that MeerKAT
pulled it off alone; earlier attempts at this kind of measurement
typically needed to be paired with an optical galaxy survey to confirm
what they were seeing.

"Detecting it directly with MeerKAT shows that this technique is
becoming a practical tool for cosmology," Paul said, calling it "a very
exciting milestone." The findings, published in The Astrophysical
Journal Letters, matter less for what they reveal about this one patch
of sky than for what they promise going forward: a cheaper, faster way
to trace how matter is distributed across billions of light-years,
which future telescopes could use to chart dark matter's scaffolding
and the universe's expansion without cataloguing galaxies by hand.
