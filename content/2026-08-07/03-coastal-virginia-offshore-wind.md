Dominion Energy's Coastal Virginia Offshore Wind project, the largest
offshore wind farm currently under construction in the United States, was
roughly 81 percent complete as of July 31, with 31 of its planned 176
turbines installed and a 32nd underway
([Offshore Wind](https://www.offshorewind.biz/2026/08/03/largest-us-offshore-wind-farm-81-pct-complete-final-turbine-expected-by-end-of-2027/)).
That 81 percent figure covers the project as a whole, not just turbines
in the water: all of the 14-megawatt Siemens Gamesa nacelles have already
been fabricated, along with about 99 percent of the towers and 85
percent of the blades. The farm has been delivering power to the grid
since March, with each turbine going live as it's finished rather than
waiting for the whole array to be done.

The honest caveat is that the finish line moved. Dominion now expects the
final turbine to go up by the end of 2027, later than the early-2027
target it had set before, citing extra contingency for weather, vessel
maintenance, and the slow work of jacking each turbine into place on the
seafloor. The capital budget has been updated to $11.65 billion. None of
that erases what's already true: a 2.6-gigawatt wind farm, once mostly a
number on a permitting document, is now a mostly-built piece of
infrastructure quietly putting clean power onto the Mid-Atlantic grid
every day, turbine by turbine.
