---
title: The US grid added more clean power last quarter than almost ever before
kicker: Climate
author: The Editors
summary: A new industry report counts 17.1 gigawatts of solar, wind, and battery storage brought online in the second quarter — 45% more than a year earlier, and the strongest such quarter on record.
order: 4
---

The American Clean Power Association's quarterly market report, released
Thursday, counts 164 utility-scale solar, wind, and battery-storage
projects that came online across the United States in the second quarter
of 2026, adding 17.1 gigawatts of capacity — 45% more than the same
quarter last year and enough to bring the country's total clean power
fleet to nearly 388 gigawatts
([American Clean Power Association](https://cleanpower.org/news/)).

The additions were broad rather than concentrated in one technology:
utility-scale solar contributed 7.4 gigawatts, battery storage another 4.7,
and land-based wind just under 5 gigawatts in what the report calls its
strongest second quarter on record, helped along by Pattern Energy's
3.65-gigawatt SunZia Wind project in New Mexico — one of the largest wind
farms ever built in the country. Utilities and corporations kept signing
up for more of it, too: clean power procurement reached 14.5 gigawatts, up
23% year over year, and the project pipeline waiting to be built swelled to
a record 205 gigawatts.

None of this happened in a friendly policy environment; the report lands
against a backdrop of federal permitting slowdowns and canceled leases for
renewable projects over the past year. That the numbers grew anyway says
something about where the economics have settled: solar, wind, and storage
are now, in enough of the country, simply the cheapest new capacity to
build, federal headwinds notwithstanding. A gigawatt of new clean power
doesn't announce itself the way a rocket launch does. It shows up as a
lower bill and a grid that copes a little better with the next heat wave —
quiet infrastructure, added at a record pace.
