---
title: A rover on Mars watched Earth vanish behind a tiny moon
kicker: Space
author: The Editors
summary: NASA's Perseverance caught our planet reduced to a single point of light before it slipped behind Phobos — the first time anyone has captured that view from another world's surface.
order: 2
---

Phobos crosses the Martian sky three times a day, and Earth is visible
from Mars for months at a stretch. Catching one directly behind the other
takes planning, and a little luck. On July 2, NASA's Perseverance rover
did both, capturing a sequence of seven images that show Earth — barely a
pixel of light from 195 million miles away — sliding behind the larger,
lumpy shape of Mars's inner moon before briefly disappearing altogether
([NASA/JPL](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasas-perseverance-rover-watches-earth-vanish-behind-martian-moon/)).

It is the first time an observation like this has been made from the
surface of another planet. Mark Lemmon, a co-investigator on
Perseverance's Mastcam-Z camera at the Space Science Institute in
Boulder, planned the shot and assembled the final composite, which NASA
processed to filter out background light and bring out the moment of
occultation in sharper detail
([NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/nasas-perseverance-captures-phobos-and-earth/)).

There is no research payload behind this one, no mystery it resolves. It
is simply a rover, built to drill rock and hunt for signs of ancient
microbial life, pausing to look up and catch our planet blink out behind
a moon barely 14 miles across. Mars has now returned the favor astronomy
has been doing from Earth for centuries — watching one world pass in
front of another — except this time the watcher is on the ground, on
another planet, and the thing disappearing is home.
