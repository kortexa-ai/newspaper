---
title: US clean power had its strongest quarter on record
kicker: Climate
author: The Editors
summary: Developers connected 17.1 gigawatts of solar, wind, and battery storage to the grid in the second quarter, and New Mexico ended Texas's long run atop the leaderboard.
order: 2
---

The American Clean Power Association's quarterly market report, released
Thursday, puts a number on a trend that's been building quietly for
years: US developers brought 164 utility-scale clean energy projects
online in the second quarter of 2026, adding 17.1 gigawatts of capacity —
45 percent more than the same quarter last year, and the strongest second
quarter the industry has recorded
([ACP](https://cleanpower.org/news/report-clean-power-maintains-strong-momentum-in-q2-2026-with-capacity-additions-up-45-year-over-year/)).

The mix was broad rather than lopsided: 7.4 gigawatts of utility-scale
solar, nearly 5 gigawatts of land-based wind, and 4.7 gigawatts of battery
storage, which alone has now passed 50 gigawatts installed nationwide.
Total US clean power capacity stands at nearly 388 gigawatts — enough,
ACP says, to supply about 83 million homes.

The geography shifted too. New Mexico added 3.6 gigawatts, enough to end
Texas's two-and-a-half-year streak as the state adding the most new
capacity, largely on the strength of Pattern Energy's SunZia project — at
3.65 gigawatts, the largest land-based wind farm ever built in the
country. Behind the current numbers sits a bigger pipeline: 205 gigawatts
of clean power capacity now under development, up 11 percent from a year
ago.

"Clean energy continues to prove itself in the marketplace," ACP's chief
policy officer, JC Sandberg, said in the report — pointing to speed of
construction and cost as much as any policy tailwind. The report notes
the growth came despite federal permitting and funding headwinds, which
makes the quarter less a story about incentives than about a technology
that has simply gotten cheap and fast enough to build anyway.
