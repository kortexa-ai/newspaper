---
title: A new crystal quietly overturns a rule about safer batteries
kicker: Science
author: The Editors
summary: Researchers in Japan found a solid electrolyte that conducts lithium ions faster than any oxide-based material on record, without the fire risk of liquid electrolytes or the toxic byproducts of sulfide alternatives.
order: 3
---

Today's lithium-ion batteries rely on a liquid electrolyte that is
flammable enough to turn a damaged battery into a fire hazard. The safer
alternative, a solid electrolyte, has long faced a tradeoff: sulfide-based
materials conduct ions quickly but can release toxic hydrogen sulfide gas
if they get wet, while oxide-based materials are safer but historically
too sluggish to compete. A team at Nagoya University, led by Professor
Takeshi Yajima with collaborator Yasutoshi Iriyama, has now closed much of
that gap
([Nagoya University](https://en.nagoya-u.ac.jp/news/articles/pr-potential-material-for-safer-li-ion-batteries-achieves-record-high-conductivity/)).

Their material, an oxyfluoride crystal called LLNOF, reached a room-
temperature conductivity of 16.3 millisiemens per centimeter — the
highest ever reported for an oxide-related solid electrolyte, and one
that starts to rival sulfides on their own turf. The mechanism behind it
is what makes the result interesting rather than merely incremental: the
prevailing assumption has been that fast ion conduction requires easily
deformable negative ions, the trait that makes sulfides quick but
hazardous. In LLNOF, the researchers found fluoride ions dynamically
shifting position as lithium moves through the crystal lattice, lowering
the energy barrier to conduction through a different route entirely. "The
general understanding has been that sulfide-based materials are better
conductors because of their anion character, but this mechanism
challenges that understanding," the team noted. The work, published in
the Journal of the American Chemical Society, offers battery designers a
new direction for solid electrolytes that are fast without being
flammable or toxic.
