NASA paused work on the Lunar Gateway station in April, redirecting the
Artemis program toward landing and building directly on the Moon's
surface instead of assembling an outpost in orbit first. That decision
left a lot of finished hardware without a mission — namely the power,
data, and mechanical systems Northrop Grumman had already built for
Gateway's HALO module.

On August 6, NASA and Northrop Grumman announced what happens to that
hardware next: three Lunar Infrastructure Demo missions, LID-1 through
LID-3, that reuse the HALO-derived systems to test what a permanent lunar
base actually needs to survive
([Northrop Grumman](https://news.northropgrumman.com/civil-space/northrop-grummans-lunar-infrastructure-demos-to-help-build-nasas-moon-base);
[Spaceflight Now](https://spaceflightnow.com/2026/08/06/nasa-northrop-grumman-repurpose-gateway-elements-for-moon-base-demo-missions/)).
The missions will prove out power management, thermal protection,
autonomy, and communications through the lunar night, when temperatures
at the Moon's south pole swing from 130°F to -334°F over two Earth weeks
of darkness — the single hardest engineering problem standing between a
handful of short visits and a base that survives.

"Our ready-to-fly, reliable HALO technologies allow NASA to move faster,
putting in place robust infrastructure that can endure the lunar night,"
said David Schiller, Northrop Grumman's vice president of civil space and
sciences. No launch dates have been set yet; the missions fall under
Moon Base Phase One, which runs through 2029. But the logic is
refreshingly plain: rather than mothball hardware that a canceled program
had already paid to build, put it to work answering the question the
next program actually needs answered.
